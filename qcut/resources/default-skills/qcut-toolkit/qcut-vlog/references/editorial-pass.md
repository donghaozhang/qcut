# Editorial Pass

## Preserve the Baseline

Use the caption-free editable master as the visual source. Never compose on top of
an already captioned export. Preserve the hard-captioned baseline as a comparison
version before building richer edits.

Keep the speaker audio continuous. Treat pacing, stickers, SFX, and B-roll as
separate concerns so each can be revised without rebuilding everything.

## Decide on the Background

Keep the real background by default. Replace it only when it is distracting,
technically broken, inconsistent with the intended tone, or explicitly rejected by
the user. Compare a representative cutout frame before committing: hair, hands,
motion blur, translucent objects, and chair edges reveal most compositing problems.

## Select Semantic Moments

Read the transcript and choose approximately 4–6 moments for a short talking-head
video. Prefer:

- the hook or surprising claim;
- a concrete example;
- a joke, reversal, or self-correction;
- a named product, benchmark, or cultural reference;
- a shift from personal experience to a larger implication;
- the final question or punchline.

Write a small cue sheet with start, end, transcript phrase, visual idea, audio idea,
and reason. If a cue has no clear reason, remove it.

## Stickers and Sound Effects

Use one sticker per concept, not a cloud of reactions. Keep stickers away from the
speaker's face and the subtitle safe area; upper corners are usually safer. Use
short entrances and exits rather than leaving a sticker parked on screen.

Choose sound effects that reinforce the semantic action: pop for an arrival, click
for a UI reference, whoosh for a transition, low impact for a revelation. Mix them
quietly beneath speech. A useful starting point is roughly 7–8% of the source SFX
level, followed by a limiter. Listen instead of trusting the number.

Preserve a no-sticker backup and a verified Sticker/SFX audio master. When a later
full-frame B-roll clip covers a sticker, suppress the sticker visually; retain its
SFX only if the sound still makes sense.

## Hand Off to B-roll

Lock and verify the Sticker/SFX version before sourcing external visuals. Keep the
cue sheet, caption-free master, and full-length audio master together. Then follow
[broll-pipeline.md](broll-pipeline.md) for rights, archival, cadence, color, and
composition rules.

## Editorial Acceptance

Compare the rich version with the clean baseline:

- Does each insert make the sentence easier or more fun to understand?
- Is the speaker still the center of the piece?
- Are any effects repetitive, late, or too loud?
- Does the edit still feel natural without seeing the cue sheet?
- Would removing an element improve the video?

Remove anything that fails the last question.
